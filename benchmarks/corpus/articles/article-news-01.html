City Council Approves Riverfront Transit Line

By Sam Okonkwo, City Desk · Updated 6:42 PM, August 3, 2026

The City Council voted 8 to 3 on Tuesday to approve the $210 million riverfront light rail extension, ending a three-year review that included two rejected designs and a contentious public hearing over the loss of a riverside parking lot.

The approved plan runs 6.4 miles from the existing Eastgate station to the new Harbor Loop, with four intermediate stops. Construction is expected to begin in spring 2027 and finish by late 2029, according to transit authority spokesperson Lena Voss.

"This is the single largest transit investment in the city in two decades," Voss said. "The route connects three neighborhoods with no current rail access."

Opposition and compromise

Councilmember Priya Anand, who voted against the measure, said the project "shortchanges the north side," where bus service was cut last year. The three no votes were split between fiscal concerns and objections to the corridor alignment.

A last-minute amendment adds $4 million for shuttle service between the Harbor Loop station and the industrial district, addressing a complaint raised by the Chamber of Commerce in May.

What happens next

The authority will now seek federal matching funds, prepare environmental filings, and begin property acquisition. A separate vote on station naming is scheduled for November.
