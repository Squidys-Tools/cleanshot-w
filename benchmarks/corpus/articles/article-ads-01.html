How to Fix a Drawer That Won't Close

By Devon Ruiz

A drawer that won't close is usually a drawer that was never level. Before you buy anything, pull the drawer out completely and look at the runners. Warped slides, a loose screw, or a single swollen panel are behind most jams.

First, check the stops. If the drawer closes but bounces back, the magnetic catch is probably misaligned by a few millimeters. Loosen the catch, slide it a fraction, and retighten. That fixes more drawers than any power tool.

Second, check the slides. Pull the drawer out, lift it from the tracks, and run a cloth over the rails to clear debris. A soft, thin coat of paste wax makes metal slides glide again.

Third, accept the weather. Wood swells in humidity, and a drawer that closed in January may fight you in August. Sand the top and bottom edges of the drawer front just enough to restore the gap, and finish with a matching wax.

If none of that works, the frame itself has drifted out of square. Check it with a carpenter's square; if it is off by more than a few millimeters, shim the cabinet before touching the drawer again.
